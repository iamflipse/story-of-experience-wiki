# The Story of Experience

Twelve lectures. About 98803 words. The speaker is not named in the sources.

## The argument, as the lectures tell it

1. **There are two ways to look.** One asks what the world is made of. The other asks how to act. He says the modern West made the first one primary, and he thinks that was the wrong pick. Action, because you are alive, is the one he calls primary. → [Two Ways](concepts/two-ways.md)
2. **Action is a path from A to B.** The basic story is a point, another point, and the behaviors that turn one into the other. The question he gives for finding the path is what beckons, asked from the admission that you do not yet know who you are. → [The Path](concepts/the-path.md)
3. **Order and chaos are a pair, not a slogan.** The yin-yang figure, he says, is serpents back to back. A dragon stands for uncertainty, danger, and opportunity, and that unknown is the ground order comes out of. What stands between you and raw nature, in the archetype lecture, is other people. He answers with one word, the patriarchy, and then unpacks it as society clothing a naked encounter. → [Balance](concepts/balance.md) · [Chaos and the Hero](concepts/chaos-and-the-hero.md)
4. **Attention itself is stacked.** Typing in Montreal, he moves from keys to words to sentences to paragraphs. That stack is his picture of a hierarchy of being, the same shape as a society whose high god carries many older names. → [Hierarchies](concepts/hierarchies.md)
5. **The story has a cost.** Sacrifice is in the pattern: Mary suffers with Christ, and the sharpest threat he names is a threat to the child. Out of chaos, in the myth lecture, comes a feminine that splits into nature as catastrophe and nature as care. → [Sacrifice](concepts/sacrifice.md)

## What these pages do not do

- They do not name the speaker. The sources do not.
- They do not treat Bilbo, Moses, Marduk, or Mary as claims about those traditions beyond the use he makes of them in the hour.
- They do not turn "the patriarchy" into a slogan he did not speak. The sentence is his, and the next sentences say what he is pointing at.
