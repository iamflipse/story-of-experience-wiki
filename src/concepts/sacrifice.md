# Sacrifice

**The unnumbered lecture puts the cost of the story on the parent, not only on the hero.**

> "Mary suffers along with Christ."
>
> [Sacrifice, Faith, and the Triumph Over Chaos](lectures/sacrifice-faith-and-the-triumph-over-chaos.md).

He says that part of the story is worth attending to. If you want the ultimate torture, you threaten the child. That, he says, is why Christ is a son, like Isaac, a child: nothing is more painful to sacrifice than a child, and that is what Mary is called upon to do. He then asks for the archetypal structure, and offers a hard consequence people actually use: a reason not to have children.

The myth lecture draws the same split on the far side of chaos, as a feminine that divides.

> "Out of chaos comes the feminine as such"
>
> [9. The Spirit of Sacrifice: Myth, Hero, and Redemption](lectures/9-the-spirit-of-sacrifice-myth-hero-and-redemption.md).

He says that emergence is bifurcated. One side is the cataclysm of nature, the other the benevolence of nature. He says nature worshippers and environmentalists place nature on the benevolent side. The caption's map language is "negative feminine" and "positive feminine," right-hand and left-hand. This page does not add a diagram the lecture only talks through.

Healing the Soul's Affliction tells a transformation story from Exodus: Moses changed by the burning bush, back to Egypt, Pharaoh doubling down, plagues, the court magicians' smoke and mirrors against what he calls the real thing, the people leaving. It is his reading of that plot as the soul's affliction and the confrontation with the abyss. The page leaves the plot in the lecture.
