# Chaos and the Hero

**The unknown, in the hero lecture, is not a blank. It is a predator-shaped sign.**

> "that's a signifier of uncertainty, danger, and opportunity."
>
> [10. The Hero's Journey: Confronting Chaos and Finding Truth](lectures/10-the-heros-journey-confronting-chaos-and-finding-truth.md).

The sign is a dragon, a serpentine figure whose core referent is predation. He calls that the unknown as such, and the ground out of which all order emerges. Order can be malevolent, he says, if the aim of the hero who meets the dragon goes wrong. The sentence is the setup. The lecture is the cases.

What keeps a person from meeting that ground naked is other people.

> "What shields you from the terrors of the natural world? The patriarchy."
>
> [8. Archetypes: Chaos, Order, and the Heroic Journey](lectures/8-archetypes-chaos-order-and-the-heroic-journey.md).

The next line is "It's you, society, nature." He says woe to you if nature is not mediated by the social, and the picture is being dropped naked and alone into a jungle. When Adam and Eve fall into chaos, God clothes them in animal skins. Clothing, in this telling, is the layer between naked vulnerability and the world. "The patriarchy" here is his name for that social layer, not a separate essay.

Agents of Transformation tells the same stacking as a history of gods. In ancient Mesopotamia the high god carries fifty names because, he says, the god is likely an amalgam of the tribal gods that came together when the civilization formed.

> "the highest god, Marduk, had 50 names."
>
> [6. Agents of Transformation](lectures/6-agents-of-transformation.md).

Tribes arrive with their own deities, then fight about whose god is on top. He says that war was sometimes a war because men are the playthings of the gods. The fifty names are the hierarchy after the fight, one figure holding many older ones.
